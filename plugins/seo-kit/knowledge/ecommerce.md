## Facts and rules (Google)

### Where product data appears
- Google lists several surfaces for ecommerce content: Search results, Google Images, Google Lens, the Shopping tab, Business Profile and Maps. Some surfaces show the same content in more than one way. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/where-ecommerce-data-can-appear-on-google)
- The **Shopping tab** needs product data in **Merchant Center**. **Lens** uses Merchant Center product data plus image best practices. **Maps** shows products only when they are uploaded with **inventory location data** (local inventory). **Business Profile** shows business details when the profile is claimed and linked with Merchant Center. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/where-ecommerce-data-can-appear-on-google)
- Merchant Center Help adds that free listings can appear on the Shopping tab, in Search rich results, Popular products (apparel, US mobile), the Shopping knowledge panel (listed countries), Images and Lens, YouTube (limited countries) and other surfaces such as Gemini, Maps and the Business Profile products module. Free listings are for **product pages only**, not home pages, policy pages or category pages. [G](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13889434)
- Beyond product data, Google suggests content that reaches shoppers at other stages: company story, seasonal offers, merchant and customer reviews, catalog and **category descriptions**, workshops and events, live streams, clear return and shipping policies and visible support contacts. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/where-ecommerce-data-can-appear-on-google)

### Structured data and Merchant Center
- Two channels, and Google recommends **both**: structured data on product pages ("where feasible"; not required to appear in Search, but it improves Google's understanding of price, discount and shipping and helps Merchant Center verify feeds) and Merchant Center product data (not required for Search results, **mandatory for some surfaces** such as the Shopping tab). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/share-your-product-data-with-google)
- Providing both "maximizes your eligibility to experiences"; some experiences **combine** markup and feed data when both exist. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product)
- Ways to send product data to Merchant Center: automatically from the online store (Google reads schema.org markup; not compatible with local inventory ads or free local listings), connecting an ecommerce platform, a file (uploaded once or fetched from a URL every 24 hours), a Google Sheets template, the product editor (few products, accounts that already have offers), the **Merchant API** (large catalogs, frequent changes). [G](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7439882)
- Smaller, rarely updated sites can start with an automated feed built from crawled pages; larger or fast-changing sites should upload feeds or use the API, because "Web crawling is not guaranteed to find all products on your site" and an API is "particularly useful for stock level updates". [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/share-your-product-data-with-google)
- **Consistency:** site and feed can drift apart (a product sold out on the site before Merchant Center updates). Google's fix is to let Merchant Center **automatically update its copy** from the website when it notices a discrepancy. Merchant Center Help asks to keep product data, including prices, consistent with the online store. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/share-your-product-data-with-google) · [G](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13889434)
- Free listings: in most cases turned on by default (Marketing › Marketing methods › Free listings card); the status does not guarantee display. A return policy should be on the website; shipping settings or costs are required in listed countries (including US, UK, Germany, France, Japan, India, Australia). Strong identifiers (GTIN, or MPN + brand) help knowledge panel inclusion. `canonical_link` matches the product to the right indexed URL; without it the `link` URL may be indexed instead. [G](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13889434)
- Google may show products crawled from the store even without markup or Merchant Center data. [G](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13889434)
- The Google pages fetched for this file do not use the name "Merchant Center Next"; the steps above are those of the current Merchant Center Help.
- Store-level markup: `Organization` (logo, contacts, identifiers, return policy for the whole business), `LocalBusiness` for physical stores (location, hours; plus Business Profile and Merchant Center store codes), `BreadcrumbList` for hierarchy, `Product`/`ProductGroup`, `Review`, `VideoObject` for product videos and live streams. The page does not map types to page types; mapping product page → `Product`, category → `BreadcrumbList`, home → `Organization` is *(inference)*. Property rules: `structured-data.md`. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/include-structured-data-relevant-to-ecommerce)

### Merchant listings and product snippets
- **Product snippets**: pages where people **cannot buy directly** (editorial reviews, comparisons); more review options (pros and cons). **Merchant listings**: pages where customers **buy from you**; more product detail options (sizes, shipping, returns). Pages with the merchant-listing required properties can also be eligible for product snippets. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product)
- Merchant listing guidelines: only pages where a shopper can purchase; pages focused on **one product** (or its variants), not category or listing pages; a **distinct URL per currency**; `Product` in the **initial HTML** for best results, because JavaScript-generated markup "can make Shopping crawls less frequent and less reliable"; price greater than zero; the merchant must be the seller (`Offer`); the Merchant Center free listings guidelines apply; Google may verify the data before showing it. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing)
- `availability` takes one value: `InStock`, `OutOfStock`, `BackOrder`, `PreOrder`, `Discontinued`, `InStoreOnly`, `LimitedAvailability`, `OnlineOnly`, `PreSale`, `SoldOut` (short names accepted). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing)
- Required properties, image sizes, shipping and return-policy markup and their precedence: `structured-data.md` (SD-22, SD-24, SD-26, SD-09).

### URL structure
- Google ignores fragments in indexing (`/product/t-shirt#black` and `#white` are one page); the same product under two URLs (`/product/black-t-shirt` and `/product?sku=1234`) cannot be recognised as one from the URLs alone; values that change (`?now=12:34am`) can look like new pages. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/designing-a-url-structure-for-ecommerce-sites)
- Rules: minimise alternative URLs for the same content; one letter case if the server ignores case; `?key=value` rather than `?value`; do not repeat a parameter (`?type=candy,sweet`, not `?type=candy&type=sweet`); descriptive words in paths (`/product/black-t-shirt-with-a-white-collar`, not `/product/3243`); no internal links to **temporary parameters** (session IDs, tracking codes, user-relative values like `location=nearby`, current time). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/designing-a-url-structure-for-ecommerce-sites)
- Same URL in internal links, sitemap and canonical (include or omit `?page=1` consistently); self-referencing canonical on every indexable page, listed in the sitemap (→ CI-22, CI-23). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/designing-a-url-structure-for-ecommerce-sites)
- Google notes that stores on an ecommerce platform can usually skip this design work. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/designing-a-url-structure-for-ecommerce-sites)

### Variants
- Every variant (combination of attributes such as colour and size) must be identifiable by **its own URL**, as a path segment (`/t-shirt/green`) or a query parameter (`/t-shirt?color=green`). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/designing-a-url-structure-for-ecommerce-sites)
- Variants selected by **optional parameters** → canonical is the URL **without** the parameter. Variants on **separate URLs** → the canonical product URL on all variant pages (`<link rel="canonical">`); the page also points to Merchant Center's `canonical_link` documentation, and using it as an alternative is *(inference)*. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/designing-a-url-structure-for-ecommerce-sites)
- `ProductGroup` markup: required `name`; group ID via `productGroupID` (or `inProductGroupWithID` on variants; equal if both); variants nested in `hasVariant` or linked with `isVariantOf`; `variesBy` limited to `color`, `size`, `suggestedAge`, `suggestedGender`, `material`, `pattern`; each variant has a **unique ID** (`sku`, `gtin`) and full `Product` markup with an `Offer` URL of its own. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product-variants)
- **Single-page** approach (all variants on one page via parameters): one distinct canonical URL for the whole group, usually the base URL with no variant preselected; every variant must be preselectable by a distinct URL that shows the right image, price and availability and can be added to the cart. **Multi-page** approach: variants are spread over "equally important pages", the `ProductGroup` has no canonical URL, each page repeats the full group definition plus stubs (`url` only) for variants on other pages. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/product-variants)
- Resolution between the two Google pages *(inference)*: default to one product page with parameter variants canonical to the base URL; use separate, self-canonical variant pages with the multi-page `ProductGroup` only when each variant page is a full landing page in its own right. Pick one model per product type and keep markup, canonical and feed (`item_group_id`, `canonical_link`) aligned with it.

### Category pages and internal links
- Google judges importance through links (how many clicks to reach a page, how many links point to it), not through URL folders. Link menus → categories → subcategories → products; link to **every product you want indexed**. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/help-google-understand-your-ecommerce-site-structure)
- Googlebot generally does not submit searches: products reachable only through the search box may never be crawled. When not every product can be linked, list them in a **sitemap or Merchant Center feed**. Links are `<a href>`, not JavaScript events on other elements (→ RJ-05). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/help-google-understand-your-ecommerce-site-structure)
- Link prominently to important items (best sellers) from the home page, blog posts or newsletters; more internal links generally signal more importance. Depth, hubs and orphan pages in general: `site-architecture.md`. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/help-google-understand-your-ecommerce-site-structure)
- Thin and empty categories: "Avoid linking to, or at least indexing, pages without useful content"; a category with no items gets `noindex`; a category removed from on-site search and browse may return `404`. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/designing-a-url-structure-for-ecommerce-sites)
- Unique copy on product and category pages: Google's ecommerce pages name "catalog descriptions and category descriptions" as useful content but set no minimum; the rule against copied manufacturer text lives in CQ-14. That a category needs some text of its own beyond the product grid is *(inference)*.

### Faceted navigation
- Facets create near-infinite URL spaces; crawlers fetch many of them before learning they are useless, which takes crawl time from new, useful URLs. [G](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/faceted-navigation)
- **If filtered URLs are not needed in Search** (often the case): block them in robots.txt and keep the unfiltered listing allowed, e.g. `disallow: /*?*color=` + `allow: /*?products=all$`; or put filters in `#` fragments, which are not crawled. `rel="canonical"` to the unfiltered page and `rel="nofollow"` are **less effective** in the long run; `nofollow` works only if **every** link to the URL has it. [G](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/faceted-navigation)
- **If some filtered URLs should be crawled and indexed**: `&` as separator (not commas, semicolons, brackets); in path-based filters a constant order and no duplicate filters; `404` for empty results, duplicate or nonsensical combinations and non-existent pagination, served at that URL, not a redirect to a shared not-found page (SPAs: follow the SPA guidance). [G](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/faceted-navigation)
- Sort and filter variations of a listing (`?order=price`) are the same list in another order: keep them out with `noindex` or robots.txt. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/pagination-and-incremental-page-loading)
- Choosing which filter combinations deserve an indexable page (for example a colour or brand filter people search for) is a business decision that Google's page leaves to the site *(inference)*. General infinite-space check: CI-34; robots.txt syntax: `crawling-indexing.md`.

### Pagination and incremental loading
- Owner: `crawling-indexing.md` › Pagination. In short: each page has a unique URL (`?page=n`) and its own canonical, never page 1; `<a href>` from each page to the next; no `#` page numbers; `rel="next"`/`"prev"` not used by Google; the same title and description may be used across the series; linking all pages back to page 1 can hint it is the best landing page. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/pagination-and-incremental-page-loading)
- "Load more" and infinite scroll are JavaScript patterns and "Google's crawlers don't 'click' buttons": back them with paginated URLs (→ RJ-22) and a sitemap or Merchant Center feed. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/pagination-and-incremental-page-loading)

### Out-of-stock and discontinued products
- **Temporarily out of stock**: keep the page live (`200`) and mark it out of stock; update `availability` in the markup and follow Merchant Center's `availability` attribute rules. Do not use the Removals tool: "customers won't be able to find first-hand information about your products on Search" and "people don't know why it's gone". [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/pause-online-business)
- Pausing sales: "Disabling the cart functionality is the simplest approach, and doesn't change anything for your site's visibility in Search." A status banner can be kept out of snippets with `data-nosnippet`. `503` only for an urgent closure of 1–2 days, never on robots.txt (→ CI-48). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/pause-online-business)
- **Permanently removed**: no replacement → `404` or `410`; moved or with a **clear replacement** → `301` to it. A page that says "not found" with `200` is a soft 404. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/troubleshoot-crawling-errors)
- Never redirect many old URLs to one irrelevant destination such as the home page: it "might be treated as a soft 404" (→ CI-21). Already indexed URLs that return `4xx` are removed from the index. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes) · [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/http-network-errors)
- The ecommerce series itself does not cover out-of-stock or discontinued products; the rules above come from the pages cited. Keeping a discontinued page live with `Discontinued` availability and links to successors, when it still answers searches, is *(inference)*.

### Reviews
- Customer reviews in markup: no fake or **undisclosed incentivised** reviews (written for money, discounts, vouchers or free products), not aggregated from other sites, readily visible on the marked-up page (→ SD-17, CQ-30). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet)
- Self-serving reviews (the store marking up reviews of itself as `Organization`/`LocalBusiness`) are ineligible for stars (→ SD-16); product reviews are eligible. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet)
- Links posted by users (reviews, Q&A, comments) → `rel="ugc"`; the attribute can be removed for trusted long-term contributors (→ CQ-09, CQ-16). Google's page names comments and forum posts; extending it to product reviews and Q&A is *(inference)*. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/qualify-outbound-links)
- Editorial reviews and buying guides written by staff, merchants or bloggers: evaluate from the user's perspective, show expertise and first-hand evidence (own photos, measurements), compare with alternatives, cover pros and cons from original research, explain why a "best" pick is best, consider links to multiple sellers; quality over length (→ CQ-29). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/write-high-quality-reviews)

### Product images
- Google does not index CSS background images: product images go in `<img src>` (with `srcset` plus a fallback `src`), near relevant text, with descriptive `alt` without keyword stuffing; high-quality, high-resolution photos (→ OP-19, OP-20). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images)
- `image` is required for merchant listings (crawlable, indexable URLs; 16x9, 4x3 and 1x1 recommended) and for product badges in Images; Lens relies on Merchant Center data plus image best practices (→ SD-09). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing) · [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/where-ecommerce-data-can-appear-on-google)

### Site search pages
- Internal search result pages are handled by the decision tree in `crawling-indexing.md` › Internal search result pages (never indexed → `Disallow`; already indexed → `noindex` first). They are not a way to get products discovered, because Googlebot does not search. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/help-google-understand-your-ecommerce-site-structure)

### Launching a new store
- Core steps: verify the site in Search Console; request indexing (few URLs → URL Inspection; many → sitemap); watch the Page indexing report; set up business details if there is a physical store; sign up for Merchant Center (required for the Shopping tab; may make products eligible for badges in Images). Merchant Center may cross-check against the index, so the site must be indexed and products purchasable. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/how-to-launch-an-ecommerce-website)
- Launch approaches: **grand reveal** (password-protected until launch, which also blocks Google; slower visibility); **home page only** ("coming soon" page, verify ownership in advance); **without availability** (full site live, products out of stock, `excluded_destination` in Merchant Center, launch date on each page; "Don't disable add-to-cart functionality", Google may use it to verify final price, tax and shipping); **soft launch** (everything live, marketing later). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/how-to-launch-an-ecommerce-website)

## Build rules
1. **URLs**: one descriptive, stable URL per product; variant URLs as path segments or `?key=value`; no session or tracking parameters in links; same URL in links, sitemap, canonical and feed (→ EC-01, EC-04, EC-19, CI-23).
2. **Variants**: choose the single-page or multi-page model per product type before building templates; emit `ProductGroup` accordingly (→ EC-02, EC-03).
3. **Catalog navigation**: menus → categories → subcategories → products, all with `<a href>`; best sellers linked from the home page; products not linkable go into the sitemap and the feed (→ EC-05, EC-06, EC-07, RJ-05).
4. **Categories**: `noindex` when empty, `404` when retired; listing pagination per `crawling-indexing.md` › Pagination (→ EC-08, EC-13).
5. **Facets**: decide per filter whether it deserves an indexable page; block the rest in robots.txt with the unfiltered listing allowed; crawlable facets follow `&`, constant order, `404` for empty results (→ EC-10, EC-11, EC-12, CI-34).
6. **Stock states**: template for out-of-stock (page live, message, `OutOfStock`), for discontinued (`301` to successor or `404`/`410`); no blanket redirect to the home page (→ EC-14, EC-15, CI-21).
7. **Product data**: `Product`/`Offer` in the server HTML from the same source of truth as the feed; Merchant Center automatic item updates on (→ EC-17, EC-18, EC-23, SD-24).
8. **Policies**: return and shipping policies on the site and in Merchant Center (→ EC-20).
9. **Reviews**: collect from verified buyers, show them on the page, `rel="ugc"` on user links, no imported or undisclosed incentivised reviews (→ EC-26, CQ-16).
10. **Images**: `<img>` with `srcset` and fallback `src`, high-resolution originals, same files in markup and feed (→ EC-27, OP-19).
11. **Launch**: Search Console Domain property, sitemap, URL Inspection on key pages, Merchant Center sign-up; remove staging `noindex`/`Disallow`/passwords on launch day (→ EC-16, CI-50, CI-38, CI-10, CI-03).

## Notes by site type

## Third-party views
- [SEJ 2026-02] Google's crawl team (Illyes and Splitt, Search Off the Record) said faceted navigation caused about 50% and action parameters (`?add_to_cart` and similar) about 25% of the crawl problems in their 2025 internal report, often injected by plugins; Googlebot cannot know a URL space is useless until it has crawled much of it, so block parameters proactively. Consistent with Google's faceted navigation page.
- [SEJ 2025-11] Google launched `ShippingService` markup (`Organization` › `hasShippingService`) for store-level shipping costs and times, with `OfferShippingDetails` for per-product overrides; Search Console and Merchant Center settings take precedence over the markup. Details: `structured-data.md`.
- [SEJ 2025-11] AI shopping features in AI Mode and Gemini (agentic checkout, calling local stores for stock) launched in the US only; they draw on the Shopping Graph, which Google described as over 50 billion listings.
- [SEJ 2026-02] Google announced the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) for agentic checkout in January 2026; it relies on Merchant Center feeds (including return policies), and from February 2026 some large retailers allowed purchases in AI Mode. No Search action beyond accurate feeds and markup; availability outside the US *(re-verify)*.
- [SEJ 2026-03] Study of product grids (Audience Key data, 4,000+ keywords): grids appeared in most commercial results, depended on Merchant Center feeds, images and price, and were independent from organic ranking. Vendor data, single study.
- [SEJ 2026-04] Opinion: price differences between feed and `Product` markup lead to disapproved items; attribute-rich titles and GTIN/MPN help. Consistent with EC-18.

## Myths and outdated advice
- ⚠ "Use `rel="next"`/`rel="prev"` for category pagination": Google no longer uses them (other engines may). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/pagination-and-incremental-page-loading)
- "Canonicalise every paginated page to page 1": do not; each page keeps its own canonical. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/pagination-and-incremental-page-loading)
- "Redirect out-of-stock or discontinued products to the home page": temporary stock-outs stay live; mass redirects to an irrelevant page can be treated as soft 404s. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes)
- "Remove sold-out products with the Removals tool": Google advises against it. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/pause-online-business)
- "Disable add-to-cart to stop orders before launch": Google may use add-to-cart to verify final prices; mark products out of stock instead. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/how-to-launch-an-ecommerce-website)
- "`canonical` or `nofollow` is enough to tame facets": both are less effective long-term than robots.txt for crawl volume. [G](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/faceted-navigation)
- "Products need Merchant Center to appear in Google Search": not required for Search results, only for some surfaces such as the Shopping tab. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/share-your-product-data-with-google)
- [SEJ 2026-07] "Without `priceValidUntil`, delivery time and return days Gemini will not include the product": one author's own audit; no Google statement *(re-verify)*.
