## Facts and rules (Google)

### Helpful, reliable, people-first content
- Google's core ranking systems aim to reward content that is **helpful, reliable and created primarily for people**, not to manipulate rankings. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Self-assessment questions (Google's list; `seo-content` uses them to review every text). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
  - **Content and quality**
    - Does the content provide original information, reporting, research or analysis?
    - Does it provide a substantial, complete or comprehensive description of the topic?
    - Does it provide insightful analysis or interesting information that is beyond the obvious?
    - If it draws on other sources, does it add substantial value and originality instead of copying or rewriting them?
    - Does the title or main heading describe the content helpfully, without being exaggerated or shocking?
    - Is this a page you would bookmark, share with a friend or recommend?
    - Would you expect to see it in, or referenced by, a printed magazine, encyclopedia or book?
    - Does it provide substantial value compared with other pages in search results?
    - Does it have spelling or stylistic issues?
    - Is it produced well, or does it look sloppy or hastily produced?
    - Is it mass-produced by, or outsourced to, many creators, or spread across a large network of sites, so that individual pages get less attention or care?
  - **Expertise**
    - Is the information presented in a way that makes you want to trust it: clear sourcing, evidence of the expertise involved, background about the author or the site (links to an author page or an "About" page)?
    - If someone researched the site that produced the content, would they come away thinking it is well trusted or widely recognised as an authority on its topic?
    - Is the content written or reviewed by an expert or enthusiast who demonstrably knows the topic well?
    - Does it contain easily verified factual errors?
  - **People-first**
    - Do you have an existing or intended audience that would find the content useful if they came directly to you?
    - Does the content clearly demonstrate **first-hand experience** and depth of knowledge (for example from having used a product or service, or visited a place)?
    - Does the site have a primary purpose or focus?
    - After reading, will someone feel they have learned enough to achieve their goal?
    - Will someone reading the content leave feeling they have had a satisfying experience?
  - **Search-engine-first warning signs** (if the answer is yes, reconsider)
    - Is the content made mainly to attract visits from search engines?
    - Are you producing lots of content on many different topics hoping some of it performs well?
    - Are you using extensive automation to produce content on many topics?
    - Are you mainly summarising what others say without adding much value?
    - Are you writing about trending topics because they are trending, not because they matter to your existing audience?
    - Does the reader feel they need to search again to get better information from other sources?
    - Are you **writing to a particular word count** because you heard Google prefers one? (It does not.)
    - Did you enter a niche without real expertise, mainly hoping to get search traffic?
    - Does the content promise an answer to a question that has none (for example an unconfirmed release date)?
    - Are you **changing the date of pages to make them seem fresh** when the content has not substantially changed?
    - Are you adding or removing a lot of content mainly to look "fresh" (this does not help ranking)?
- Besides self-assessment, ask honest feedback from trusted people **not affiliated** with the site, and examine the pages that lost the most traffic. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Content for generative AI features: prefer **non-commodity** content built on direct experience (for example a first-hand review instead of a summary; "why we dropped…" instead of "7 tips for…"). Guiding principle: would visitors find it satisfying? [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- Reviews: the **reviews system** (it applies in several languages; see the page for the current list) rewards in-depth analysis, original research and direct experience. It evaluates articles, blog posts and pages of recommendations or opinions published by the site, **not user reviews** on a product page. It works mainly page by page, and site-wide when a site publishes many reviews. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/reviews-system)
- High-quality reviews: the user's point of view, expertise, **evidence** (photos, audio, links) of direct experience, quantitative measurements, comparisons, pros and cons, alternatives, reasons behind "best for…" picks; quality and originality, not length. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/write-high-quality-reviews)

### Who, how, why
- **Who**: is it self-evident who created the content? Is there a byline where readers would expect one? Does it lead to a page with details about the author? Google **strongly encourages** accurate authorship information. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- **Fictional authors** are deceptive: invented names, AI-generated photos and false credentials make the page untrustworthy. This explicit warning was reported by SEJ as a 1 October 2026 update of the English guide and is not yet in the Google documentation we could read. [SEJ 2026-10] *(re-verify)*; page to re-check: [creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- **How**: explain the process (for reviews: how many products were tested, results, photos). For automated or AI-generated content: a visible disclosure where readers would expect it, with details on how and why automation was used. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- **Why**: the main purpose must be to help people. Using automation or AI **primarily to manipulate rankings** violates the spam policies. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

### E-E-A-T: what it is and what it is not
- E-E-A-T = experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trustworthiness; **trust is the most important part**; a piece of content does not need to show all of them. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- **It is not a specific ranking factor**: the systems use a mix of factors that identify content with good E-E-A-T, and give more weight to strong E-E-A-T on **YMYL** topics (health, financial stability, safety, the welfare of society). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)
- **Quality raters** do not control rankings and their data is not used directly in the algorithms: it serves as feedback on the systems. The rater guidelines (section 4.6.5 scaled content abuse, 4.6.6 low effort, originality and added value) are useful for self-assessment. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) [G OH 2023-07](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/july)

### AI-generated content
- Generative AI is useful for research and for structuring original content; generating **many pages without added value** can violate the scaled content abuse policy. Content must meet Search Essentials and the spam policies. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- Accuracy, quality and relevance also apply to **metadata**: `<title>`, meta description, structured data (follow the guidelines and validate), alt text. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- **Manual fact-checking**: models predict word sequences and can be wrong; it is "critical" to check all AI content and its metadata by hand before publishing. Reported by SEJ as a 1 October 2026 update of the English guide, not yet in the Google documentation we could read. [SEJ 2026-10] *(re-verify)*; page to re-check: [using generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- Give context: explain how the content was created; use image metadata. Ecommerce (Merchant Center): AI images carry the IPTC `DigitalSourceType` value `TrainedAlgorithmicMedia`; AI-generated product titles and descriptions are specified separately and labelled. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- AI drafts with editorial review: using tools is not a problem, overall quality is what counts (and it is not automatically high). Low-quality machine translations: have them reviewed by native speakers or set `noindex`. Poor AI content published without review: remove it or fix it. [G OH 2024-06](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2024/june) [G OH 2023-09](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/september)
- Copying or AI-rewriting other sites' content violates the spam policies. [G OH 2022-11](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2022/november)

### Content and generative AI features in Search
- AEO and GEO "are still SEO": AI Overviews and AI Mode rely on the core ranking and quality systems. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- **Do not create separate pages for every query variant or fan-out sub-question** to manipulate results: that is scaled content abuse. Seeking inauthentic mentions is of little use. There is no ideal length, chunking is not needed, no rewriting "for AI". [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- **Manipulating generative AI responses** in Search falls within Google's definition of spam. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)

### Ranking systems
- The systems work **mostly at page level**, with some site-wide signals (which alone do not decide how every page ranks). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide)
- Active systems Google describes: BERT, crisis information systems, **deduplication**, exact match domain (little credit), freshness, link analysis and PageRank, local news, MUM (specific uses), neural matching, **original content**, **removal-based demotion** (many valid copyright, defamation, doxxing and similar requests), **passage ranking**, RankBrain, reliable information, **reviews system**, **site diversity** (generally no more than 2 results from the same site at the top; subdomains count as the same site), **spam detection** (SpamBrain). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide)
- **Retired** systems, folded into others: helpful content (merged into the core systems in **March 2024**), Panda (2015), Penguin (2016), Hummingbird. Since March 2024 there has been no single signal or system for helpfulness. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies)
- Third-party "authority" scores (DA, DR and similar) **do not correspond to any Google signal**. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies)
- Sections very different from the main site can be treated as standalone sites (without the site-wide signals): a drop is not necessarily a penalty. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/11/site-reputation-abuse)
- Discover got its own core update (February 2026): expertise is assessed **per topic**; a single isolated article on a topic is not enough. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update)

### Core updates
- Several times a year, announced on the Search Status Dashboard; they do not target specific sites; a drop does not mean a violation: other content has become more relevant. There are also **smaller, continuous, unannounced core updates**: improvements can be rewarded without waiting for a named update. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates)
- Diagnosis: wait for the rollout to finish and then **at least one full week**; compare with a week before the start; analyse Web, Image, Video and News separately; look at the top pages and queries. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates)
- Small drop (for example **from position 2 to 4**): no drastic action. Large drop (for example **from 4 to 29**): an in-depth assessment of the **site as a whole** with the self-assessment questions. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates)
- Avoid "quick" changes based on rumours; make substantial, sustainable improvements; **deleting content is the last resort**. Effects can take days or **several months**, sometimes until the next core update; there are no guarantees. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates)
- Named updates are mostly about transparency; whatever is actionable is published in documentation and policies. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/help/status-dashboard)

### Spam updates and SpamBrain
- The anti-spam systems (including **SpamBrain**, AI-based) are always running; notable improvements are announced as spam updates. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/spam-updates)
- Recovery is possible once the systems observe compliance **over a period of months**. For **link spam**, the benefit those links gave is neutralised and **does not come back** after the fix. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/spam-updates)

### Spam policies (full list)
Policy names follow Google's English spam-policies page.
- **Definition**: techniques used to deceive users or manipulate Search, **including generative AI responses**. Detection is automated and, where needed, human review leads to a manual action; consequences are lower rankings or removal. Google can also act on practices not listed. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Cloaking**: showing users and search engines different content to manipulate rankings (for example keywords only for the search engine's user agent). A paywall is **not** cloaking if Google sees the protected content and the flexible sampling guidelines are followed. Serving 410 to Googlebot and 200 to users, or linking variants only for Googlebot, is cloaking. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) [G OH 2023-04](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/april) [G OH 2024-06](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2024/june)
- **Doorway abuse**: several sites with small variations of URL or home page; **several pages or domains targeting regions or cities that funnel users to one page**; pages built to funnel visitors; substantially similar pages that look like search results rather than a browsable hierarchy. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Expired domain abuse**: buying an expired domain and reusing it mainly to manipulate rankings with low-value content (for example casino content on a former school site). Using an old domain for a new, original, people-first site is fine. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies)
- **Hacked content**: injected code, pages or content (hidden text or links), malicious redirects, including ones conditioned on referrer, user agent or device. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Hidden text and link abuse**: white text on a white background, text behind images, text positioned off-screen with CSS, **opacity or font size 0**, a link hidden in one tiny character. **Allowed**: tabs, accordions, slideshows, tooltips, text for screen readers only. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Keyword stuffing**: lists of phone numbers with no added value, **blocks of cities and regions** the page tries to rank for, unnatural repetition. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Link spam**: buying or selling links for ranking (money, goods, services, products in exchange for a review with a link); excessive link exchanges and partner pages; automated link building; links required by contracts without the option to qualify them; text ads that pass ranking credit; advertorials, **guest posts** or press releases with optimised anchors; low-quality directories and social bookmarking; links embedded in widgets; **widely distributed links in footers or templates of many sites**; forum signatures; low-value content created for links. Advertising links are allowed with `rel="nofollow"` or `rel="sponsored"`. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Machine-generated traffic**: automated queries to Google and scraping of results without permission. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Malicious practices**: malware, unwanted software and **back button hijacking** (manipulating browser history or functions so the user cannot go straight back to the previous page; for example inserting never-visited pages into history, or unsolicited ads or recommendations). **In force since 15 June 2026**; it can come from third-party libraries or ad platforms; consequences are manual actions or automated demotions; after the fix, request reconsideration. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/04/back-button-hijacking)
- **Misleading functionality**: fake generators, sites that promise tools (PDF merger, timer, dictionary) and lead to ads instead. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Scaled content abuse**: many pages generated mainly to manipulate rankings, **however they are created** (AI, people, a mix): AI output without added value, scraping disguised with synonyms, translation or obfuscation, aggregation without value, several sites to hide the scale, nonsense pages stuffed with keywords. If you host such content, exclude it from Search. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies)
- **Scraping**: republishing without added value or attribution, copying with minimal changes, reproducing feeds, embed-only sites with no value. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Site reputation abuse** (the section is headed "Site reputation policy" on Google's page): third-party content published on a host **mainly to exploit its ranking signals**. Third-party content alone is not a violation. Not violations: wire services and press releases, news syndication, UGC, forums and comments, columns and opinion pieces, advertorials meant for readers, properly used affiliate links, ad units. Google assumes individual pages (even new ones) reflect the quality of the domain. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
  - Human review weighs 4 factors, none necessary or sufficient on its own: **presentation** consistent with the host, **quality** compared with the domain, **authorship** stated or implied, **identical content** on several sites. Examples: a coupon section integrated with a disclaimer → action unlikely; an affiliate article without an author, not reachable from navigation and copied → action likely; a signed freelance piece with editorial oversight → unlikely. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
  - **Since 30 August 2026**: outside the EEA a manual action hits the affected pages; **in the EEA** there is no manual-action effect, but the pages can be **ranked separately** from the main domain (not automatically); earlier manual actions in the EEA were lifted; there is no obligation to use `noindex`; in the EEA there is a fast reconsideration path and out-of-court dispute settlement (CEDR). Notifications arrive in the Manual actions report and the Message center. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/08/update-site-reputation-policy)
  - History: introduced in March 2024 (enforced from 5 May 2024), tightened on 19 November 2024 (no level of first-party oversight changes the third-party nature of the content; moving it to a subfolder or subdomain of the same site does not fix it). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/11/site-reputation-abuse)
- **Sneaky redirects**: showing search engines one piece of content and sending users elsewhere; mobile-only redirects to spam. Legitimate: site moves, merging pages, post-login redirects. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Thin affiliation**: descriptions and reviews copied from the merchant. Good affiliate content adds price information, original reviews, testing, comparisons. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **User-generated spam**: spam accounts, posts and comments. Prevention: `rel="ugc"`/`nofollow` on untrusted links, `noindex` on new users' content until they have earned trust, moderation, CAPTCHA, blocklists. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/prevent-abuse)
- **Other practices that can lead to demotion or removal**: **legal removals** (demotion after many valid legal removal requests); **personal information removals** (sites with exploitative removal practices); **policy circumvention** (new sites, subdomains or subdirectories created to keep violating); **scam and fraud** (impersonating businesses, fake customer support, fake contact details). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Structured data that does not comply with the guidelines can lead to a manual action and loss of rich-result eligibility; do not aggregate reviews from other sites into the markup. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) [G OH 2024-08](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2024/august)

### Manual actions and spam reports
- **Only manual actions are notified** in Search Console (Manual actions report, Message center); algorithmic actions are not. After the fix: a **reconsideration request**, which can take **several weeks**. [G OH 2022-12](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2022/december) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/help/site-position-in-search-faq)
- User spam reports can be used for manual actions; their text is sent to the site owner; reports containing personal data are not processed. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/help/report-quality-issues)
- Bought domain: check the Manual actions report in Search Console (expired or resolved actions do not appear); the algorithms can react to quality problems even without a manual action. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/help/crawling-index-faq) [G OH 2023-08](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/august)
- Disavow: only if you **built or paid for** links you cannot remove, or after a manual action; not for "odd" links flagged by tools. Random spam links and negative SEO are generally ignored. [G OH 2022-11](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2022/november) [G OH 2023-01](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/january) [G OH 2024-06](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2024/june)
- The owner is responsible for what SEO providers do; never insert a link to the SEO provider's site. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo)

### Recovering from traffic drops
- Tools: Search Console Performance report (up to **16 months**; beyond that, the API or the BigQuery export), the Data anomalies page, Google Trends. Compare the last 3 months with the previous period and with the year before; examine queries, pages, countries, devices, search appearance; filter by search type; sort pages by click difference. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/debugging-search-traffic-drops)
- Typical causes: algorithmic update, technical problem (server, robots.txt, 404, wrong `noindex`: a slower decline), security (Security issues report), spam (Manual actions report), seasonality or changing interest, migrations (a few weeks), reporting anomalies. Stable impressions with falling clicks = weak title or snippet, or competitors with rich results. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/debugging-search-traffic-drops)
- Recommended order *(inference)*: 1) rule out technical and measurement problems; 2) check Manual actions and Security issues; 3) compare dates with the Search Status Dashboard; 4) if the drop matches an update, apply the core-update procedure; 5) improve the site as a whole, not random single pages. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/help/status-dashboard)
- Deleting poor pages does not automatically make the site better: the site must have value of its own. Algorithmic demotions for thin content take **several months** to be reassessed. [G OH 2022-11](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2022/november) [G OH 2022-12](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2022/december)
- If Google does not index pages, or drops them, after requests, the systems are often not convinced of the site's value: improve overall quality and uniqueness instead of resubmitting. [G OH 2023-09](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/september)

## Audit checks
Severity follows `conventions.md`. Many checks need human judgement: the skill flags candidates, it does not hand down verdicts.

| ID | Check | How to verify | Severity | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CQ-01 | Manual actions and Security issues reports are clean | Search Console (if the user grants access); otherwise ask the user | Critical | [G](https://developers.google.com/search/help/site-position-in-search-faq) |
| CQ-02 | No "photocopy" pages for cities, regions or variants that funnel to the same service (doorway abuse) | Crawl: clusters of pages with near-identical text (high similarity) and URL patterns like `/service-[city]` | Critical | [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) |
| CQ-03 | No mass production of low-value pages (templates, programmatic pages, one page per query variant) | Count pages per template; share of unique text vs boilerplate; publishing spikes in the history (git, sitemap `lastmod`) | Critical | [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) |
| CQ-04 | No keyword stuffing: blocks of cities or regions, lists of phone numbers, unnatural repetition | Regex for lists of place names in footer or body; abnormal n-gram frequency | Critical | [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) |
| CQ-05 | No manipulative hidden text or links | CSS: `font-size:0`, `opacity:0`, negative `text-indent`, colour = background, off-screen positioning on text with keywords or links (tabs, accordions and screen-reader text excluded) | Critical | [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) |

## Build rules
For builders and writers. Each rule points to the checks it satisfies.
1. **Reader first**: define the audience, the site's purpose and the question each page answers; a page exists because it serves someone, not to catch a query variant. → CQ-03, CQ-24, CQ-27 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
2. **Direct experience and evidence**: real cases, own photos and data, the method explained; avoid summaries of what the top results already say. → CQ-23, CQ-29 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
3. **Real author**: byline, author page with verifiable expertise, an About page; never invented authors, photos or credentials. → CQ-20, CQ-21 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
4. **AI as a tool, not a factory**: use it for research and structure; every generated text and metadata field is checked by hand before publishing; disclose its use where readers expect it. → CQ-22, CQ-37 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
5. **No pages in series**: no page per city, variant or sub-question with the same text; local pages only where there is a real location or service with distinct content. → CQ-02, CQ-03 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
6. **Length is free**: write as much as the user needs; no word-count threshold or keyword density; use the audience's language naturally. → CQ-04 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) [G OH 2023-01](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/january)
7. **Honest titles**: descriptive, without exaggeration or clickbait. → CQ-26 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
8. **Update for real**: change the date only with substantial edits; update or remove what is no longer valid. → CQ-25 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)
9. **Paid links or links given in exchange for something** always carry `rel="sponsored"`/`nofollow`; no link exchanges or guest posts for links; the site builder's link on client sites without commercial anchors (preferably `nofollow`). → CQ-09, CQ-10, CQ-11 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
10. **Third-party content** only if integrated: same design, stated author and editorial responsibility, commercial disclosure, reachable from navigation. → CQ-12, CQ-32 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
11. **No script that manipulates browser history** (routers, pop-ups, ad libraries included). → CQ-08 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/04/back-button-hijacking)
12. **Same content for users and Googlebot** (also with prerendering or SSR): no "bot" versions. → CQ-06 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
13. **Reviews and testimonials** only if real; markup only if visible on the page; no reviews aggregated from other sites. → CQ-30 [G OH 2024-08](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2024/august)
14. **YMYL topics**: verifiable expertise, sources, caution (no promises you cannot keep). → CQ-28 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
15. **After a drop**: no emergency changes based on rumours; diagnose with data, then make substantial improvements to the whole site. → CQ-33, CQ-34 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates)
16. **User-generated content** (comments, forums, reviews, listings): moderation, `rel="ugc"` on user links, `noindex` on new users' content until it earns trust, anti-bot on forms; internal search pages handled as in `crawling-indexing.md`. → CQ-16, CQ-17 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/prevent-abuse)

## Notes by site type
**ecommerce**
- **Product descriptions** are the shop's own, not copied from the manufacturer or the supplier feed: copied text is thin affiliation or scraping, and Acme Widgets gains nothing by publishing the same paragraph as every other reseller. Add what only the shop knows (sizing notes, use cases, photos, answers to customer questions). → CQ-14 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Category pages** need useful text and a real selection, not short, repeated, auto-generated blurbs; near-empty or near-identical categories (one per filter or colour) behave like pages in series. → CQ-03, CQ-31 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) [G OH 2023-09](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/september)
- **Scaled AI descriptions**: generating thousands of product or category texts without review is scaled content abuse whatever the tool; review a sample by hand, check facts and metadata, and in Merchant Center mark AI images with IPTC metadata and label AI-generated titles and descriptions. → CQ-03, CQ-22 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **User reviews policy**: the reviews system does not assess user reviews on product pages; review markup is still bound by the structured data policies: only real reviews, never bought, exchanged or incentivised without disclosure; review markup only for reviews visible on the page; no reviews imported from other sites into markup. → CQ-30 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/reviews-system) [G OH 2024-08](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2024/august)
- Buying guides and "best X" pages follow the high-quality reviews guidance (evidence of use, criteria, pros and cons); affiliate and partner links are qualified. Coupon or partner sections only if integrated and curated. → CQ-09, CQ-12, CQ-29 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/write-high-quality-reviews) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- `marketplace-ugc` modifier (marketplaces, sellers' listings, Q&A, open reviews): treat every user-supplied field as a spam vector. Moderate new sellers, listings and reviews; put `rel="ugc"` (or `nofollow`) on user links; keep new accounts' content `noindex` until it earns trust; use CAPTCHA and blocklists on forms; watch for listings that host off-topic or spam content to exploit the domain. → CQ-16, CQ-09, CQ-17, CQ-32 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/prevent-abuse) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)

**editorial**
- Byline + author page; expertise per topic (Discover assesses it per topic); no off-topic articles written for traffic. → CQ-20, CQ-27 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update)
- Reviews with evidence of use; affiliate content with added value and qualified links. → CQ-29, CQ-09 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/write-high-quality-reviews)
- Comments and forums moderated, with `rel="ugc"` on user links (same rules as the `marketplace-ugc` modifier above). → CQ-16 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/prevent-abuse)

**local-business**
- **No cloned city pages** and no blocks of city lists: doorway abuse and keyword stuffing. Pages per location only if real and distinct (Northwind Physio gets one page for its Oslo clinic, not twenty for nearby towns it does not serve from a location). → CQ-02, CQ-04 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Real reviews, never exchanged for links or services; local directories do not improve SEO (at most they bring direct traffic). → CQ-30 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) [G OH 2022-11](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2022/november)

**personal-brand**
- "Who" is the core: an About page with real name, role, skills, links to profiles; case studies that show the "how" (decisions, results, screenshots, demos). → CQ-20, CQ-23 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Non-commodity content (real problems solved, not "10 tips for…"). Hosting sponsored or third-party articles to "grow the domain" exposes the site to the site reputation abuse policy. → CQ-12, CQ-32 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- "Built by" links on client sites: `nofollow` or a neutral anchor, never commercial anchors spread across sites. → CQ-10 [G OH 2023-01](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/january)

**professional-services**
- Verifiable credentials (professional registers, certifications, issuing bodies); topics close to YMYL (health, finance, work and careers) need more trust. Treating "adjacent" topics this way is *(inference)*. → CQ-28 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- Service-area pages follow the same doorway rule as local-business: one page per real service or location with distinct content, never one clone per town. → CQ-02 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Real reviews and testimonials only, never exchanged for links or services. → CQ-30 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)

**saas-product**
- "X vs Y", "alternatives to X" and glossary pages are the templates most often produced at scale (see the Lily Ray study under Third-party views): publish them only with honest comparisons and evidence of use, not one generated page per competitor. → CQ-03, CQ-29 [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)

## Third-party views
- [SEJ 2026-10] *(re-verify)* On 1 October 2026 SEJ reported two Google documentation updates not yet visible in the Google pages we could read: the generative-content guide calls manual fact-checking of text and metadata "critical", and the people-first guide warns against fictional authors. Search Liaison had already said (January 2024) that a byline alone does not improve ranking: it serves trust and transparency.
- [SEJ 2026-05, Lily Ray/Amsive, observational study of 220+ sites] Sites boosted by AI content platforms: 54% lost ≥ 30% from their peak, 39% ≥ 50%. Templates at risk: "A vs B" comparisons, "what is X" glossaries, "best X for Y", self-promotional listicles, "alternatives to…" pages, programmatic city or language pages, FAQ farms. Correlation, not causation; consistent with the scaled content abuse policy.
- [SEJ 2025-12 / 2026-06, Google news + analyses by Solís, SISTRIX, Amsive] Core updates of December 2025 (11–29 December), March 2026 (27 March–8 April) and May 2026 (21 May–2 June): in these analyses specialised sites, official sites, owners of the service and sites matching intent gained; generalists and aggregators lost. Third-party interpretations on partial samples.
- [SEJ 2026-03→09, Google news] Spam updates in 2026: March (24–25 March, under 20 hours), June (24–26 June), August (18–21 August), September (started 24 September; Google said the rollout could take up to two weeks); no new policy was announced. On 15 May 2026 Google clarified that manipulating generative responses is spam.
- [SEJ 2026-09, Mueller] Recovery after large updates takes **months**; old low-value programmatic pages can still weigh on the site's trust; adding more generated text does not make them helpful; disavow files are processed over months.
- [SEJ 2026-07, Mueller/Splitt, podcast] "Crawled – currently not indexed" is only sometimes about quality, but when the systems are concerned about overall quality they reduce crawling and indexing; look at the big picture, not single pages.
- [SEJ 2026-07, Mueller/Splitt] Indexable internal search pages are a spam vector (sometimes even flagged as "hacked"); how to handle them is decided by the internal-search decision tree in `crawling-indexing.md`.
- [SEJ 2026-07, news on the review snippet doc] Fake reviews, or incentivised reviews not disclosed (including in markup), can lead to a manual action.
- [SEJ 2026-04, Google doc news] Spam reports can now lead to manual actions and their text is forwarded to the site (consistent with Google's page on spam reports).
- [SEJ 2026-02, Marie Haynes, 4 cases without a control group] Recoveries after the December core update attributed to real trust, original content and UX: causation not proven.
- [SEJ 2025-12, Montti quoting Mueller] Against a common practice: E-E-A-T is not something to "add" to pages; real signals count (demonstrable experience, external recognition). Consistent with Google.
- [SEJ 2025-11, Forrester, opinion] Visible author and credentials, a consistent bio across site and profiles: in line with Google's "who". ⚠ The claims about AI "universal verifiers" and "citation-ready" schema are speculative and not to be recommended.
- [SEJ 2026-06/09, Google papers S-CTS and SAFE] Research systems for detecting clusters of mass-produced AI content: Google has not said they are used in web ranking; the practical message stays "no mass production".
- [SEJ 2026-07, forum case] Partial "thin content" manual action on a forum whose AI chatbot answered as a staff member: the cause is a hypothesis, not confirmed by Google.
- [SEJ 2026-08, social media reports] ⚠ "The spam update hits AI content": anecdotes on small samples, unsupported by Google; for Google the problem is manipulative purpose, not the tool.
- [SEJ 2026-04/09, news] Search Console impression-logging error from 13 May 2025 to 27 April 2026 (clicks not affected): in drop analyses use clicks and treat 27 April 2026 as a discontinuity.
- [SEJ 2025-12, rollerblader, opinion] There is no optimal keyword density; flag stuffing, do not measure density. ⚠ Tools' "optimisation scores" are not to be recommended.

## Myths and outdated advice
- **"You need a minimum word count"**: no minimum or maximum length; Google has no preference. Word count does not define thin content. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) [G OH 2022-11](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2022/november)
- **Optimal keyword density**: it does not exist; using the users' terms explicitly is enough. [G OH 2023-01](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/january)
- **Heading order and number as a factor**: they do not matter for Search. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)
- **"E-E-A-T is a ranking factor to optimise"**: it is not a specific factor; raters do not directly affect ranking. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- **"AI content is penalised because it is AI"**: purpose and value count; manipulation at scale is spam whatever the tool. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)
- **"Recovering from the Helpful Content Update"**: since March 2024 it has not been a separate system. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide)
- **"You must wait for the next core update to recover"**: smaller updates run continuously; improvements can be rewarded earlier (though it often takes months). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates)
- **"A core update drop is a penalty"**: it is not; manual penalties are notified in Search Console. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates)
- **Penalty for internal duplicate content**: there is no manual action for internal duplicates; copying from other sites is a different matter. Translated or localised content is not duplicate. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) [G OH 2024-06](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2024/june)
- **Changing the date or adding/removing content to look "fresh"**: does not improve ranking. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- **"Deleting weak pages lifts the site"**: not automatically; deletion is the last resort. [G OH 2022-11](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2022/november) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates)
- **Optimal publishing frequency**: it does not exist. [G OH 2022-12](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2022/december)
- **DA/DR, "spam score", text-to-code ratio**: not Google signals. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies) [G OH 2024-08](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2024/august)
- **Pre-emptive disavow of "toxic" links flagged by tools**: not recommended; random spam links are ignored. [G OH 2022-11](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2022/november)
- **Directories, social bookmarking, buying backlinks, guest posts for links**: a waste of time or a policy violation. [G OH 2023-05](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/may) [G OH 2023-09](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/september)
- **"Overseeing third-party content is enough to avoid site reputation abuse"**: superseded since November 2024. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/11/site-reputation-abuse)
- **"Site reputation abuse is fixed with `noindex` or by moving the section to a subdomain"**: moving it within the same site does not fix it; since 30 August 2026 there has been no manual-action effect in the EEA and `noindex` is not required. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/11/site-reputation-abuse) [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Many `noindex` pages or internal `nofollow` links = low quality; "index bloat"**: no. [G OH 2024-08](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2024/august) [G OH 2023-06](https://developers.google.com/search/help/office-hours/2023/june)
- **Google Ads improves organic ranking**: no. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/help/site-position-in-search-faq)
- **AEO/GEO as a separate discipline, `llms.txt`, chunking, rewriting for AI, "built" mentions**: Google says it is still SEO and these tactics are not needed. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
