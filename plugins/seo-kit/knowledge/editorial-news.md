## Facts and rules (Google)

### Article pages and markup
- Article markup uses one of three schema.org types: `Article`, `NewsArticle`, `BlogPosting`. It has **no required properties**; the recommended ones are `author` (with `author.name` and `author.url`), `datePublished`, `dateModified`, `headline` and `image`. It helps Google show a better title, image and date for the article. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article)
- **No markup is required** to be eligible for Google News features; Article markup only describes the content more explicitly. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article)
- `headline`: prefer a concise title, because long titles may be truncated on some devices. Title and description lengths in general: see `on-page-appearance.md` Build rules. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article)
- `image`: crawlable and indexable URLs, images that represent the article (not logos or captions), in a format Google Images supports; Google recommends several high-resolution images of at least 50,000 pixels (width × height) in 16x9, 4x3 and 1x1. Property checks: SD-09. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article)
- **Multi-page articles**: `rel="canonical"` on each page points to that page or to a view-all page, **not to page 1** of the series. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article)
- Google may need several days after publishing to find and crawl a page. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article)
- Verification: Rich Results Test (URL mode) and the URL Inspection tool; for paywall markup Google notes that the Rich Results Test cannot catch every issue, such as spammy content or spammy markup. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/paywalled-content)

### Authors and publisher
- In Article markup: include **every author shown on the page**, one `author` object per author (never merged), only the name in `author.name` (no job titles, honorifics, publisher names or "posted by"), `Person` for people and `Organization` for organizations, with a valid `url` or `sameAs`. Owned by SD-19; Person/ProfilePage for the author page are owned by `entity-personal-brand.md` (EN-02, EN-06, EN-13). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article)
- Google News transparency: readers "want to trust and understand who writes and publishes the content they read". Sites should show clear **dates and bylines**, information about the **authors, the publication and the publisher**, information about the **company or network** behind the content, and **contact information**. [G](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/6204050)
- Google News policies on ads and sponsored content: paid material must not exceed the publisher's own content; sponsored content must not be presented as independent editorial and the sponsorship must be disclosed. Previews must not promise what the content does not deliver. [G](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/6204050)
- Violations can lead to removal from news surfaces, and repeated or serious violations to loss of eligibility; manual actions appear in Search Console under Security and Manual actions. [G](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/6204050)
- Fictional authors and credentials: see CQ-21 and EN-14. Byline and author page: CQ-20, EN-13.

### Publication and update dates
- The byline date is Google's estimate of when the page was published or updated. Google combines several signals because each one can be wrong; it does not guarantee that a date will appear in results. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/publication-dates)
- Show a **visible, clearly labelled date** near the article ("Published 4 February 2026", "Updated 14 February 2026, 8 pm CET"); a publication date, an update date, or both. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/publication-dates)
- In structured data use a `CreativeWork` subtype with `datePublished` and/or `dateModified`, in ISO 8601 format (as in the Google example; the Article page names ISO 8601). The date is required, the time is not; Google recommends adding time and time zone (without one, Google uses Googlebot's time zone), with daylight saving time handled correctly. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/publication-dates) · [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article)
- Visible and structured dates must be **consistent**; time and time zone may be omitted from the visible text. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/publication-dates)
- **No future dates**, and never the date of an event the article describes (use Event markup for events). **Minimize other dates** on the page; if Google picks the wrong date, remove some or all of the other dates. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/publication-dates)
- The publication-dates page says nothing about artificially refreshing dates. The rule against changing dates without substantial edits comes from the helpful content guidance and is owned by CQ-25.

### News sitemap
- News publishers can use a News sitemap to tell Google about news articles: either news tags added to the existing sitemap or a separate News sitemap (a separate file allows better tracking in Search Console). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/news-sitemap)
- Include **only articles created in the last two days**. Older URLs: remove them, or remove their `<news:news>` block. An empty News sitemap causes no problem in Search (Search Console may show an "Empty sitemap" warning). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/news-sitemap)
- Up to **1,000 `<news:news>` tags per sitemap**; split into several sitemaps beyond that. Only one `<news:news>` per `<url>`. **Do not create a new sitemap for each update**: add new articles to the existing file. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/news-sitemap)
- Google News help: News sitemaps "are distinct from Google Search sitemaps" and help the crawler find content faster. [G](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/9606634)
- General sitemap rules (format, size, canonical URLs, `lastmod`, submission): `crawling-indexing.md` CI-08, CI-13 to CI-16; submission: ME-03 in `measurement.md`.

| Tag | Rule |
|---|---|
| `<news:news>` | Parent of the other news tags; one per `<url>` |
| `<news:publication>` | Contains `<news:name>` and `<news:language>`; one per `<news:news>` |
| `<news:name>` | Must match exactly the publication name shown on news.google.com, without anything in parentheses |
| `<news:language>` | ISO 639 code, two or three letters; exceptions `zh-cn` and `zh-tw` |
| `<news:publication_date>` | W3C format (`YYYY-MM-DD`, `YYYY-MM-DDThh:mmTZD`, with seconds or fractions); the **original** date and time of first publication |
| `<news:title>` | The article title as on the site; no author name, publication name or date |

### Google News and Top stories
- Google "automatically considers all web content for inclusion in Google News, so you don't need to apply"; content must comply with the overall requirements and content policies. Publishers are also automatically considered for **Top stories** and the **News tab** of Search. Eligibility is not a guarantee of ranking. [G](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/9606538) · [G](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/9607025)
- To be eligible, content should follow best practices for article pages and must not violate Search policies, spam policies or feature policies. [G](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/6204050)
- **Publisher Center** is not a submission gate: Google describes it as a tool to configure monetization (Reader Revenue Manager) and Google News Showcase settings, publication and organization settings, user access and website property verification. It is not a CMS. [G](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/9606538)
- Google News ranking factors listed by Google: relevance, prominence, authoritativeness, freshness, usability, location and language. Google News "aims to promote original journalism", accepts no payment for ranking, and ad programs have no effect on ranking. [G](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/9606702)
- **Googlebot-News** has no user agent string of its own (it crawls with the Googlebot strings); its robots.txt token affects Google News (news.google.com and the Google News app). Blocking Googlebot-News excludes the content from Google News. [G](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/google-common-crawlers) · [G](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/9607025)
- Local news systems surface local sources in Top stories and Local news; reliable-information systems elevate quality journalism; original content systems help show original reporting ahead of pages that only cite it. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide)
- **Preferred sources**: readers can pick a site as a preferred source; it is then more likely to appear in Top stories with a "preferred" badge (globally), and can carry the badge in AI Mode and AI Overviews if the site is included in Search generative AI features in Search Console. Only domains and subdomains qualify (not `/blog` subdirectories). A button script or a deep link `https://www.google.com/preferences/source?q=<domain>` can invite readers; using them is not required. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/preferred-sources)
- Measurement: the Search Console **Google News** performance report covers news.google.com and the Google News app, not the News tab of Search; the News tab is in the Search results report with Search type = News. [G](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/10083653)
- Multilingual news sites: do not automatically redirect visitors to other language versions. [G](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/9606634)

### Discover
- Content is eligible if it is indexed and meets Discover's content policies; **no special tags or structured data are required**; eligibility does not guarantee appearance. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)
- Images: large images are enabled by `max-image-preview:large` (or AMP). Use images **at least 1,200 px wide**, more than **300,000 pixels** in total, ideally **16x9**; if you crop by hand, keep the important detail in the landscape crop. Set the preferred image with schema.org markup or `og:image`, and avoid generic images such as the site logo. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)
- Titles and headlines should capture the essence of the content. Avoid clickbait and similar tactics that inflate engagement; avoid sensationalism that plays on morbid curiosity, titillation or outrage; do not use misleading or exaggerated previews or withhold information needed to understand the content. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)
- Discover may not recommend job applications, petitions, forms, code repositories, or satire without context. Policy violations can lead to Discover manual actions in Search Console. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)
- Content that is timely, tells a story well or gives unique insight does well; older content can appear when it is helpful. Discover uses many of the signals Search uses for helpful, people-first content, plus an overall good page experience. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)
- Traffic is less predictable than keyword search and should be treated as supplemental; changes can come from user interests, content types, Search updates, or reasons unrelated to quality or publishing frequency. The Discover performance report shows impressions, clicks and CTR for 16 months, once a minimum impression threshold is reached. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)
- **Discover core update (5 February 2026)**: the first core update dedicated to Discover showed more locally relevant content from sites based in the user's country, less sensational content and clickbait, and more in-depth, original and timely content from sites with expertise on the topic. Expertise is assessed topic by topic (a local news site with a dedicated gardening section can have expertise in gardening; a single article on a topic does not establish it). It rolled out first to English-language users in the US, with expansion to other countries and languages announced for the following months. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update)

### Paywalls and flexible sampling
- Paywall structured data helps Google **tell paywalled content apart from cloaking**. Set `isAccessibleForFree: false`, and add `hasPart` with a `WebPageElement` whose `isAccessibleForFree` is `false` and whose `cssSelector` points to the paywalled section; **only `.class` selectors**; several sections → one `WebPageElement` per class; do not nest sections. Supported on `CreativeWork` and subtypes. Apply it to AMP and non-AMP versions. Markup check owned by OP-42. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/paywalled-content)
- Googlebot (and Googlebot-News, if applicable) must be able to access the page for paywalled content to be indexed; with `amp-subscriptions`, the authorization endpoint must grant access to the right bots, and the bot access policy must be the same on AMP and non-AMP pages. If the content should not reach the browser at all, use a paywall that does not send it (client-side JavaScript paywalls: see RJ-33). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/paywalled-content)
- Spam policies: a paywall is **not cloaking** when Google can see the full content behind it, as any person with access could, and the site follows the flexible sampling guidance. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Flexible sampling**: *metering* (a quota of free articles; monthly metering is preferred to daily) or *lead-in* (part of the article shown; showing the first sentences after the meter runs out is a good practice). For most daily news publishers the right value is "between 6 and 10 articles per user per month"; Google suggests starting with **10 articles per month** for Search users and iterating. User satisfaction degrades significantly when paywalls are shown more than 10% of the time; even small changes to sampling can affect ranking. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/flexible-sampling)
- Snippet control on paywalled pages: `data-nosnippet` and `max-snippet` (see OP-09, OP-10); AI Overviews and AI Mode follow the same preview controls. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/paywalled-content)

```json
{"@context": "https://schema.org", "@type": "NewsArticle", "headline": "…",
 "isAccessibleForFree": false,
 "hasPart": {"@type": "WebPageElement", "isAccessibleForFree": false, "cssSelector": ".paywall"}}
```

### Web Stories
- Web Stories are the web version of the "Stories" format (video, audio, images, animation, text, tap-through). They can appear as a single result in Search (all regions and languages where Search is available) and as a card in Discover (most likely in the US, India and Brazil). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/enable-web-stories)
- Technical requirements: a **valid AMP** story; on every story the fields `publisher-logo-src`, `poster-portrait-src`, `title` and `publisher`; the story must be **canonical** (`rel="canonical"` to itself; language versions via localized-version annotations); not blocked by robots.txt or `noindex`; linked from the site or listed in the sitemap. Verify with the Web Stories Google Test Tool, URL Inspection, or the AMP Linter. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/enable-web-stories)
- Best practices: video first (under 15 seconds per page; the page also gives 60 seconds as a maximum without saying whether per page or per story); about 280 characters of text per page; titles under 90 characters, ideally under 70; poster at least 640x853 px (3:4) without burned-in text; logo at least 96x96 px (1:1); alt text and captions; link stories from the home page or category pages. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/web-stories-creation-best-practices)
- Content policy: no copyright infringement; not **text heavy** (stories may be ineligible when most pages have more than **180 characters**); no stretched or pixelated assets; a binding theme or narrative; complete without sending users elsewhere for essential information; not solely advertising; affiliate links only as a minor part. Egregious violations can remove a site from richer Search experiences permanently. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/web-stories-content-policy)
- The best-practices page (about 280 characters) and the content policy (ineligibility when most pages exceed 180 characters) give different figures; the policy threshold is the one with consequences, so the audit uses 180 *(inference)*.
- When they make sense *(inference)*: visual, sequential stories (a recipe, a trip, a match recap) produced with real video or photos. They are an optional format; a text news site does not need them, and they add a separate AMP production pipeline to maintain.

### Site reputation abuse and scaled content
- **Site reputation abuse**: third-party content published on a host site "mainly because of that host's already-established ranking signals". Third-party content alone is not a violation. **Not** abuse: wire services and press releases, news publications syndicating content from other publications, UGC and comment sections, columns and opinion pieces, native advertising or advertorials meant for readers, affiliate links treated appropriately, third-party ad units. A freelancer's article with clear authorship and editorial oversight is a worked example Google rates "unlikely" to be actioned. Examples of abuse: sponsored payday-loan reviews hosted by an educational site; a low-quality casino page on a medical site. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Since **30 August 2026** (announced 28 August 2026): outside the EEA a manual action under this policy affects search results for the affected portion of the site (the rest is not affected); inside the EEA the manual action has no effect, but the affected section may be separated in Google's systems so that over time it ranks independently. Notifications and reconsideration requests continue through Search Console, and eligible sites can bring disputes to mediation. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/08/update-site-reputation-policy)
- There is no obligation to `noindex` content under a manual action outside the EEA, and not doing so is not taken into account for ranking inside the EEA; earlier EEA manual actions under this policy were lifted. Creating new subdomains, subdirectories or sites to keep violating policies is itself circumvention. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) Full treatment: `content-quality-spam.md` (CQ-12, CQ-32).
- **Scaled content abuse**: many pages generated mainly to manipulate rankings, however they are produced, including "using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users", scraping or rewriting feeds, stitching content together, or many sites hiding the scale. Google recommends excluding such content from Search. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) Checks: CQ-03, CQ-22, CQ-37.

### Archives, tag pages and syndication
- Pagination of archives and categories (self-canonical, crawlable, no `Disallow` or `nofollow`), infinite scroll and internal search: owned by `crawling-indexing.md` (Pagination section, CI-34) and RJ-22 / OP-41.
- Syndication: the canonical link element "is not recommended for those who want to avoid duplication by syndication partners"; "the most effective solution is for partners to block indexing of your content". [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization-troubleshooting) Original content systems try to show original reporting ahead of pages that only cite it. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide)
- Tag and topic archives: the fetched Google pages say nothing specific about them. Treating near-empty or near-duplicate tag pages as low-value pages is an *(inference)* from the duplication and helpful content guidance (CQ-31).

### Freshness: news vs evergreen
- "Query deserves freshness" systems show newer content when a query calls for it (for example, a recent earthquake). [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide) Google News lists freshness among its ranking factors. [G](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/9606702)
- *(inference)* News articles keep their original `datePublished`; corrections and developments update `dateModified` and the visible "Updated" label; a substantially new development gets a new article linking to the earlier one. Evergreen guides are revised when facts change, and only then get a new `dateModified`. Freshness is a property of the query, not a reason to touch dates (CQ-25).

## Build rules
1. Article template: one `NewsArticle` (news) or `BlogPosting`/`Article` (blog, guides) node, server-rendered, with `headline`, several large `image` URLs, `datePublished`, `dateModified` and one `author` object per visible author linking to the author page. → ED-01, ED-02, SD-19, SD-20
2. Visible, labelled dates near the headline, consistent with the markup, with time zone in the markup; no other prominent dates; `dateModified` changes only with substantive edits. → ED-04, ED-05, ED-06
3. Publisher pages from day one: about or masthead, contact, author pages with real biographies; sponsored content labelled in a fixed, visible position. → ED-07, ED-08
4. News publishers: one News sitemap file at a stable URL, regenerated on publish with the last two days only, at most 1,000 entries, the `news:name` exactly as on news.google.com; also listed in robots.txt `Sitemap:` and submitted in Search Console. → ED-09, ED-10, ED-11, CI-08, ME-03
5. Do not block Googlebot-News unless the publisher chooses to leave Google News; Publisher Center is optional for appearing. → ED-12
6. Every article has a lead image of at least 1,200 px wide, set as `og:image` (→ OP-22); also supply a 16x9 version, as Google's Article markup guidance recommends (Facts › Article pages and markup; OP-22 does not check the crop); `max-image-preview:large` site-wide; headlines describe the story without bait. → OP-22, ED-15, ED-16
7. Paywall: wrap paid sections in a class, declare them with `isAccessibleForFree` + `hasPart`/`cssSelector`, give Google's crawlers the full text (verify crawler identity by IP, not user agent alone: CI-46 *(inference)*), start with monthly metering around 10 free articles. → ED-17, ED-18, ED-19
8. Web Stories only with real visual material and a maintained AMP pipeline. → ED-20, ED-21, ED-22
9. No partner or white-label sections hosted for their ranking value; no mass-produced articles, AI-assisted or not. → ED-23, ED-24, CQ-22
10. Syndication contracts require partners to block indexing of copies; pagination per `crawling-indexing.md`; tag pages created only for topics with enough articles. → ED-25, ED-26

## Notes by site type
- **editorial**: everything in this file applies. Audit order: indexing and rendering first, then ED-01 to ED-08 (articles, dates, transparency), then News sitemap and Googlebot-News (news publishers only), then Discover, paywall (if any), Web Stories (if any), spam risks. Use the fixed Discover and Google News reports (ED-13) as the baseline for every later report. Example: Daily Fjord (`dailyfjord.example`) lists only the last two days of articles in its News sitemap and keeps evergreen guides in the regular sitemap.
- **professional-services**: a blog (for example on studioferri.example) needs `BlogPosting` with author and dates (ED-01, ED-02, ED-04, ED-06) and Discover-ready images if posts are timely (ED-14). News sitemaps, Googlebot-News, flexible sampling and Web Stories rarely apply: a services blog is seldom news *(inference)*. Avoid hosting guest posts or sponsored sections for their link or ranking value (ED-23).
- **saas-product**: changelogs, release notes and the product blog are articles: ED-01, ED-04, ED-06 apply; announcement posts can reach Discover with large images (ED-14). Scaled "use case" or comparison articles generated with AI are the main risk (ED-24, CQ-03). News sitemap only if the company runs a real newsroom.
- **paywall (modifier)**: add ED-17, ED-18 and ED-19 to any archetype with subscriber-only content; check that free sampling, markup and crawler access stay consistent after every paywall vendor change.

## Myths and outdated advice
- "You must apply to Google News or be approved in Publisher Center to appear in Top stories" → Google considers all web content automatically; Publisher Center manages monetization, Showcase and settings. [G](https://support.google.com/news/publisher-center/answer/9606538)
- "NewsArticle markup (or AMP) is required for Google News or Top stories" → no markup requirement for Google News features; the fetched Google News pages list no AMP requirement. AMP remains one way to get large images in Discover, alongside `max-image-preview:large`. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article) · [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)
- "Put every article ever published in the News sitemap" → only the last two days. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/news-sitemap)
- "Update the date to stay fresh in Discover and Top stories" → no benefit; owned by CQ-25 and `myths-deprecated.md`.
- "A cross-domain canonical protects the original from syndication partners" → not recommended; partners should block indexing. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization-troubleshooting)
- "Showing Googlebot the full paywalled article is cloaking" → not when Google sees what any subscriber sees and the paywall is marked up and follows flexible sampling. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- "Moving a third-party section to a subdomain or subdirectory fixes site reputation abuse" → creating new subdomains, subdirectories or sites to keep violating the policies is circumvention. Supervision of third-party content: see `content-quality-spam.md` and `myths-deprecated.md`. [G](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
